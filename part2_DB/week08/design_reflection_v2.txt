1. What changed the most for me was fine tuning a lot of the details and fixing a lot of issues I had from my first draft. For example, I originally had made entering the coordinates unrestricted meaning you could enter anywhere on the world, now it is constrained to the TBS area which should hopefully prevent any errors. Setting the foreign keys in this draft was also a change I made which now prevents orphan records. Sitting down and thinking about the logic and construction of my database prompted these changes.  
2. The hardest constraint for me was the person delete rule. Because my work includes humans, privacy and consent rules should probably result in using CASCADE as some individuals may not want to be recorded. Although, this does not protect against accidental deletes with critical date, therefore I am still deciding between these two categories.
3. Many things in my design cannot be enforced such as IRB consent. I think this is a real challenge in my work and theoretical thought as ethnography and ecology become hard to intertwine. More specifically, ethnography is often times unquantifiable so deciding what is reflected in the database is a precarious situation. Moreover, putting strong limitations to an ethnographic data could severly confine what is represented. Future dates also are unenforceable because I dont think CHECK can actually prevent an earlier date.
4. Because of privacy issues, I followed the privacy rules outlined in the book where I have implemented a surrogate person_id for community members instead of their names, since my database could be shared and a number simply lets participants be recorder under a pseudonym without their name being littered throughout the database. Additionally, I used RESTRICT for most of my foreign keys so records that other data depends on cannot be deleted by accident as outlined in chapter 10.
